# lead_HS

## What this project is about

Paints and varnishes sold in the European Economic Area (EEA; German: EWR —
the EU countries plus Iceland, Liechtenstein and Norway) fall into two customs
categories for this study:

- **3208** — paints based on synthetic or modified natural polymers, in a
  solvent base (solvent-borne), and
- **3209** — the same kind of polymer paints, but water-based.

The question: **which of these products contain lead?**

Paint tins do not carry an ingredient list the way food packages do. But for
almost all professional products there is a **safety data sheet** (SDS;
German: *Sicherheitsdatenblatt*) that must name hazardous ingredients above
certain thresholds. Lead compounds — historically used as colour pigments,
rust-inhibiting additives and drying agents — are among the things that must
be declared.

The plan in three steps:

1. **Understand the market.** How many distinct paint products are there in
   the EEA at all? No official register counts them, so the total has to be
   estimated by combining several sources (poison-centre notifications,
   Nordic product registers, industry and trade statistics).
2. **Check a statistically sound sample.** Roughly 2,000–3,000 products
   across all market segments — do-it-yourself, professional and industrial
   paints, and imports — by reading their safety data sheets.
3. **Verify in the laboratory.** A small number of physical samples is tested
   to check what the sheets cannot show: lead traces below the declaration
   threshold.

The outcome is a **research report plus a reusable database** of checked
products and their lead status.

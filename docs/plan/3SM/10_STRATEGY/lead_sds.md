# Lead identification via SDS — compounds, legal status, feasibility

## Abstract

This document collects what is known about lead in paints and where the law
stands. Lead compounds appear in paints as colour pigments (e.g. lead
chromate yellows/reds, white lead), rust-protection additives (red lead) and
drying agents in solvent-based alkyd paints (lead octoate). EU law bans
several of them in paints or for consumer use, but rust-protection additives
and drying agents remain legal; Switzerland bans all paints with ≥ 0.01%
total lead (100 ppm) via ChemRRV Anhang 2.8, shielded from EU-lawful imports
by the VIPaV exceptions catalogue (Cassis-de-Dijon context). Safety data
sheets must list classified hazardous ingredients above 0.1% — so they
reveal deliberately added lead, but not the 100–1000 ppm band that matters
for the Swiss ban. The document also lists the first hard EU-market
presence findings and prior studies that calibrate expectations.

## EU legal frame (verified)

- Annex XVII 16/17: lead carbonates/sulfates banned **in paints** ("where
  the substance or mixture is intended for use as paint"); member states may
  permit use/placing on the market for **restoration and maintenance of
  works of art and historic buildings** (ILO Convention 13) — the hook under
  which artists' lead colours survive; practice varies by country.
- Annex XIV 10–12 + entries 28–30: lead chromates post-sunset (2015);
  DCC/DCL authorisations annulled (GC T-837/16, 2019), effects maintained
  until fresh decision (CJEU C-389/19 P, 2021), **all 8 uses refused
  (Commission decision announced 17 Mar 2022)** → no lawful EU supply since;
  OJ reference still to be pulled.
- Entry 63: lead ≥0.05% in mouthable consumer **articles** (paint is a
  mixture → context); PVC amendment 2023/1422 unverified.
- SDS cutoffs (Reg (EU) 2020/878, Annex II REACH): CMR 1A/1B ≥ 0.1% must be
  listed in Sec. 3; OEL-listed substances must be listed (no explicit % for
  that route); ranges permitted. SDS free of charge on request (Art. 31(8)).

## Swiss layer (VIPaV / ChemRRV / Cassis de Dijon)

- **Cassis de Dijon (CH):** EU/EEA-lawful products may enter the Swiss
  market without Swiss re-approval (THG Art. 16a, unilateral, 1 July 2010);
  exceptions catalogued in **VIPaV (SR 946.513.8) Art. 2**.
- **VIPaV Art. 2 Bst. a Ziff. 1** = "bleihaltige Anstrichfarben und Lacke
  sowie damit behandelte Produkte (Anhang 2.8 ChemRRV)" — the first item of
  the exceptions catalogue, in force since 2010, still in the SECO
  Negativliste (1 Jan 2026); enforcement: BAFU. (SECO e-mail's "Art 2a(1)"
  read as this item — no Art. 2a exists; inference.) **Art. 16 VIPaV** =
  list-maintenance provision (context only).
- **ChemRRV Anhang 2.8** (2005 wording; current text OPEN): lead paint =
  total Pb ≥ 0.01% (100 ppm); ban on placing on the market incl. treated
  articles; no standing lead exceptions (transitions expired 2006/07).
  Stricter than the EU — hence the exception.
- Five-yearly review of the catalogue (VIPaV Art. 3): 2023 outcome "keep"
  (Eignung/Erforderlichkeit/Verhältnismässigkeit affirmed); next ~2028.
- Chemicals/CdD boundary: a product enters either via Swiss chemicals law
  or via CdD, not mixed; follow-up duties survive (Anmeldestelle Chemikalien).
- ChemO/REACH-alignment details beyond this frame remain an open dossier
  item (see 10_STRATEGY/MASTER open items).

## Feasibility assessment

- **Visible:** intentionally added classified lead compounds ≥ 0.1% (pigments at % levels; driers typically ≥0.1% Pb — flagged as domain inference).
- **Invisible — headline caveat:** the Swiss ban engages at **100 ppm total
  Pb**, the EU SDS floor is **1000 ppm (0.1%) for classified compounds**: a
  paint can be EU-lawful, fully documented, and still exceed the Swiss ban
  without the sheet showing it. Plus the usual blind spots: impurity lead,
  under-declaring, stale sheets (NO 2014: 320 SDS generic/incomplete;
  artificial-stone 2022: constituents under-reported).
- **No laboratory in this project:** blind spots cannot be bounded
  experimentally. Mitigation is document-only corroboration (see
  `methodology.md`): cross-check positives against TDS, labels, retailer
  listings, older SDS versions, cross-market brand variants; report
  contradictions as findings; carry an explicit limitations section in the
  final discussion basis.
- **Availability:** manufacturer/brand sites + B2B portals (scrape; DE/FR/IT/EN); no public EU SDS repository; PCN submissions not public; UFI in Sec. 1.1 as join key.
- **CN assignment from SDS:** medium (aqueous?) + binder (acrylic/vinyl/polyester/other) → CN8; borderline: ≥50%-solvent polymer solutions (3208 10/20 vs 90), hybrids. Artists' colours → 3213 (census annex).

## Prior studies (calibration)

- **No EU/EEA-wide or Swiss lead-in-paint market survey exists** — the gap this study fills (the EU-market stream findings above are the first seed records).
- Mexico 2026 (213 paints, lab): 55% >90 ppm; 91% of leaded ≈ lead chromate (Pb:Cr 4:1); 12 leaded products labelled "no lead". DOI 10.1093/annweh/wxag023
- IPEN global campaign (59 countries, >5,000 paints, lab): high prevalence in developing markets; country database Oct 2024. ipen.org/our-work/lead-paint
- Kumar et al. 2018 review: often >10,000 mg/kg in LMIC paints. DOI 10.1016/j.envint.2018.08.052
- Turner & Filella 2022: European road paints (above).
- WHO 2024: 48% of countries have binding lead-paint controls.
- Working hypothesis (to test): Swiss consumer decorative ≈ 0% declared lead; niche documented presence in marine/anticorrosive (3208) and artists' colours (3213); third-country imports higher; lead driers unknown.
